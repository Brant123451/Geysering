\section{Governing model}
\label{sec:model}

The governing equations below and the numerical discretization in
Section~\ref{sec:model:numerics} are taken directly from the companion methods
manuscript submitted in parallel \cite{xue2026model}.
The same state variables, closures, regime limits, moving-front conditions,
and conservative update are retained. The derivations, nonlinear-solver
details, boundary catalogue, and canonical verification suite are omitted.
\subsection{Decoupled two-fluid formulation}
\label{sec:model:framework}

For a stratified gas--liquid section, the cross-sectionally averaged model is
written
\begin{equation}
\begin{gathered}
\frac{\partial\mathbf U}{\partial t}
+\frac{\partial\mathbf F}{\partial x}=\mathbf S,\\[2pt]
\mathbf U=
\begin{bmatrix}
A_g\rho_g\\ \rho_gQ_g\\ A_l\\ Q_l
\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
\mathbf F=
\begin{bmatrix}
\rho_gQ_g\\
\rho_gQ_g^2/A_g+P_gA_g\\
Q_l\\
Q_l^2/A_l+\tfrac12\Lambda_dA_l^2
\end{bmatrix},\\[3pt]
\mathbf S=
\begin{bmatrix}
0\\
P_g\,\partial_xA_g-A_g\rho_g g\cos\theta\,\partial_xh_l+\mathbf S_g\\
0\\
\mathbf S_d
\end{bmatrix}.
\end{gathered}
\label{eq:system}
\end{equation}
Here $A_k$, $Q_k$, $u_k=Q_k/A_k$, and $\rho_k$ are phase area,
discharge, velocity, and density, with $A_g+A_l=A_f$. Subscripts $g$ and $l$
denote gas and liquid, and $A_f$ is the undeformed full-pipe area.\par
\begin{samepage}
\noindent Resolved gas is isothermal, $P_g=\rho_gRT=c_g^2\rho_g$, with
$c_g=\sqrt{RT}$.
\end{samepage}
The regular source vector
$\mathbf S$ separates the geometric gas-pressure and hydrostatic coupling
shown explicitly above from the axial-gravity, wall-friction, and interfacial-
shear contributions collected in $\mathbf S_g$ and $\mathbf S_d$. The full
constitutive correlations are deferred to the companion manuscript.

For a circular conduit of diameter $D$, the liquid central angle $\gamma$
gives the liquid area $A_l=D^2(\gamma-\sin\gamma)/8$ and depth
$h_l=D[1-\cos(\gamma/2)]/2$. The local free-surface width is
$\partial A_l/\partial h_l=D\sin(\gamma/2)$, and the remaining gas area is
$A_g=A_f-A_l$. Consequently,
$\partial h_l/\partial A_l=(\partial A_l/\partial h_l)^{-1}$, which supplies
the local hydrostatic geometry in both the stratified and vented
free-surface limits.

The liquid restoring coefficient in Eq.~\eqref{eq:system} is
\begin{equation}
\Lambda_d
=\frac{2gH_g}{A_l}
+\frac{\rho_l-\rho_g}{\rho_l}g\zeta
-\frac{\rho_g(u_g-u_l)^2}{\rho_lA_g},
\qquad
\zeta=\frac{\cos\theta}{D\sin(\gamma/2)}.
\label{eq:restoring_coefficient}
\end{equation}
$H_g$ is gas-pressure head, $\theta$ is the pipe inclination, $D$ is pipe
diameter, and $\gamma$ is the liquid central angle. The three terms represent
gas pressure, hydrostatic--buoyancy restoration, and the inviscid
Kelvin--Helmholtz slip contribution.

\subsection{Multi-regime reductions of the decoupled system}

The same area--discharge variables recover three hydraulic limits without
changing the underlying definition of phase inventory. First, when $A_g=0$ and
$A_p\simeq A_f$, the liquid-full branch evolves
$\mathbf U_p=[A_p,Q_p]^{T}$ according to
\begin{equation}
\begin{gathered}
\partial_tA_p+\partial_xQ_p=0,\\[3pt]
\partial_tQ_p+
\partial_x\!\left(\frac{Q_p^2}{A_p}+gA_fh_s\right)=\mathbf S_p,
\end{gathered}
\label{eq:liquid_full_branch}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf S_p$ is the regular pressurized-branch momentum source and
$\mathbf R_p=[0,\mathbf S_p]^{T}$ is the corresponding two-component
source vector. Its pressure is supplied by the elastic-pipe closure
\begin{equation}
P_p-P_{\mathrm{ref}}
=\rho_la^2\frac{A_p-A_f}{A_f},
\qquad
h_s=\frac{P_p-P_{\mathrm{ref}}}{\rho_lg},
\label{eq:elastic}
\end{equation}
where $P_{\mathrm{ref}}$ is the reference pressure, $a$ is the water-hammer
wave speed, $h_s$ is the piezometric head, and $u_p=Q_p/A_p$ below denotes the
liquid-full velocity.

Second, in a vented reach $P_g\simeq P_{\mathrm{atm}}$ and hence
$H_g=\mathrm{const.}$ With negligible gas inertia, the limiting relations
\mbox{$\rho_g/\rho_l\rightarrow0$} and
\mbox{$\Lambda_d\rightarrow g\cos\theta/(\partial A_l/\partial h_l)$} hold,
and the liquid equations
take the following \mbox{Saint--Venant form}:
\begin{equation}
\begin{gathered}
\partial_tA_l+\partial_xQ_l=0,\\[3pt]
\partial_tQ_l+
\partial_x\!\left(\frac{Q_l^2}{A_l}
+\frac{g\cos\theta}{2(\partial A_l/\partial h_l)}A_l^2\right)=\mathbf S_d,
\end{gathered}
\label{eq:free_surface_branch}
\end{equation}
The geometric derivative $\partial A_l/\partial h_l$ is frozen at the local
state when the reduced flux is differentiated. Thus
Eq.~\eqref{eq:free_surface_branch} is the locally frozen-coefficient
Saint--Venant-type limit used by the companion algorithm.
Third, on the stratified side of a nearly full
($A_l\rightarrow A_f$) moving front, the slip term is negligible but
distributed gas mass and momentum remain governed by the first two components
of Eq.~\eqref{eq:system}. The liquid equations then take the near-front
mixed-flow limit
\begin{equation}
\begin{gathered}
\partial_tA_l+\partial_xQ_l=0,\\[3pt]
\partial_tQ_l+
\partial_x\!\left[
\frac{Q_l^2}{A_l}+gA_fH_g
+\frac12\frac{\rho_l-\rho_g}{\rho_l}g\zeta A_l^2
\right]=\mathbf S_d.
\end{gathered}
\label{eq:near_front_branch}
\end{equation}
This last relation is a local near-full reduction of the four-variable branch,
not a separate two-equation replacement for the gas dynamics.

The stratified liquid subsystem is hyperbolic only for $\Lambda_d>0$; the
unmodified sign of $\Lambda_d$ remains the instability diagnostic. For
$\rho_g\ll\rho_l$ and negligible gas-pressure variation, the local inviscid
critical-slip threshold is exceeded when
\begin{equation}
(u_g-u_l)^2>
\frac{\rho_l-\rho_g}{\rho_g}\,g\zeta A_g.
\label{eq:critical_slip}
\end{equation}
Beyond this threshold the inviscid short-wave problem is locally ill posed
\cite{stuhmiller1977}. This sign change is an instability indicator, not a
wave-breaking, entrainment, or liquid-bridge model; the face regularization
introduced below does not alter that diagnostic. Thus the gas-acoustic,
free-surface gravity-wave, elastic water-hammer, and moving mixed-flow-front
responses belong to one parent architecture, although they do not share a
single state dimension or pressure law. Lumped sealed-pocket and
apparatus-specific pipe--shaft closures are declared separately; they are not
additional parent-PDE regimes. The numerical subset and reductions used by each
reported calculation are identified with that campaign.

\subsection{Regime-transition Riemann problem}
\label{sec:model:front}

Let $x=x_\Gamma(t)$ denote a moving pressurized--stratified front, with
$w_\Gamma=\dot x_\Gamma$. Pressurized flow is placed on the left and
stratified flow on the right; reflection gives the reverse orientation. Only
liquid mass and momentum enter the jump. The adjacent gas enters through its
kinematic condition and the restoring coefficient $\Lambda_{d,\Gamma}$.

For a zero-thickness front with no liquid storage or concentrated liquid
force, integration over a control volume that shrinks onto the front gives
\begin{equation}
\mathbf F_{p,\Gamma}-w_\Gamma\mathbf U_{p,\Gamma}
-\left(\mathbf F_{f,\Gamma}-w_\Gamma\mathbf U_{f,\Gamma}\right)
=\mathbf 0,
\label{eq:front_relative_balance}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf U_{p,\Gamma}=[A_{p,\Gamma},Q_{p,\Gamma}]^{\mathrm T}$ and
$\mathbf U_{f,\Gamma}=[A_{l,\Gamma},Q_{f,\Gamma}]^{\mathrm T}$. Expanding
Eq.~\eqref{eq:front_relative_balance} gives the liquid mass and momentum
Rankine--Hugoniot conditions:
\begin{equation}
\begin{gathered}
Q_{p,\Gamma}-Q_{f,\Gamma}
=w_\Gamma\left(A_{p,\Gamma}-A_{l,\Gamma}\right),\\[4pt]
\left(\frac{Q_{p,\Gamma}^2}{A_{p,\Gamma}}+gA_fh_{s,\Gamma}\right)
-\left(\frac{Q_{f,\Gamma}^2}{A_{l,\Gamma}}
+\frac12\Lambda_{d,\Gamma}A_{l,\Gamma}^2\right)
=w_\Gamma\left(Q_{p,\Gamma}-Q_{f,\Gamma}\right).
\end{gathered}
\label{eq:front_jump}
\end{equation}

The liquid-full branch cannot receive gas. The moving-frame gas condition is
therefore
\begin{equation}
\left(Q_g-w_\Gamma A_g\right)_\Gamma=0
\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad
u_{g,\Gamma}=w_\Gamma.
\label{eq:front_gas_condition}
\end{equation}
Here $A_{g,\Gamma}=A_f-A_{l,\Gamma}$. Bounded extrapolation from the nearest
active stratified gas cells, together with the isothermal gas characteristic
and state equation, provides $\rho_{g,\Gamma}$ and $P_{g,\Gamma}$; the nearest
admissible state is used if extrapolation fails.

Because the water-hammer speed greatly exceeds the front speed, one
pressurized acoustic characteristic reaches the front. Its compatibility
relation is
\begin{equation}
u_{p,\Gamma}-u_{p,L}
+\frac{g}{a}\left(h_{s,\Gamma}-h_{s,L}\right)=0.
\label{eq:front_pressurized_compatibility}
\end{equation}
The reduced closure takes the stratified liquid trace from the adjacent state,
\begin{equation}
\left(A_{l,\Gamma},Q_{f,\Gamma}\right)=\left(A_R,Q_R\right),
\label{eq:front_stratified_trace}
\end{equation}
and freezes $\Lambda_{d,\Gamma}$ at that state.

Let $s_-=u_{l,R}-c_{l,R}$ and $s_+=u_{l,R}+c_{l,R}$, where
$c_{l,R}=[\max(\Lambda_{d,R}A_R,0)+c_{\mathrm{num}}^2]^{1/2}$. The admissible
front-speed ranges are
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\text{slow:}\quad   & w_\Gamma\le s_-,\\
\text{middle:}\quad & s_-\le w_\Gamma\le s_+,\\
\text{fast:}\quad   & w_\Gamma\ge s_+.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:front_speed_ranges}
\end{equation}
After Eq.~\eqref{eq:front_stratified_trace} fixes the stratified trace, the
unknowns are $w_\Gamma$, $A_{p,\Gamma}$, and $Q_{p,\Gamma}$. Equations
\eqref{eq:front_jump} and \eqref{eq:front_pressurized_compatibility} determine
them within each speed range. Positivity, speed ordering, and momentum-residual
tolerance determine acceptance; bracketed iteration is retained as the
fallback. The full residual construction and nonlinear solution are given in
the companion methods paper.

Figure~\ref{fig:cut_cell_geometry} shows the moving control-volume geometry
used by the front-tracking update. Regular cells are updated in conservative
finite-volume form,
\begin{equation}
\mathbf U_j^{n+1}=\mathbf U_j^n
-\frac{\Delta t}{\Delta x_j}
\left(\widehat{\mathbf F}_{j+1/2}
-\widehat{\mathbf F}_{j-1/2}\right)
+\Delta t\,\mathbf S_j.
\label{eq:fv_update}
\end{equation}
Fifth-order WENO-Z reconstruction is used in smooth regions
\cite{borges2008}. Stencils adjoining a moving front, a boundary, or a newly
formed regime use second-order minmod MUSCL--TVD reconstruction
\cite{vanleer1979}; area and gas-density traces are limited toward their cell
averages to preserve $0<A_l<A_f$ and $\rho_g>0$.

The two phases use branch-appropriate face problems rather than a single HLL
or Rusanov flux. The gas flux consists of an area-aware quasi-one-dimensional
isothermal-Euler entropy-conservative core and characteristic entropy
dissipation \cite{winters2020,chan2024}. The liquid flux is sampled from a
two-wave shallow-water Riemann solution \cite{toro2001}, using a positive
face-regularized restoring coefficient:
\begin{equation}
\begin{gathered}
\widehat{\mathbf F}_g
=\mathbf F_g^{\mathrm{EC}}
-\tfrac12\mathbf K_g(\mathbf v_{g,R}-\mathbf v_{g,L}),\\[3pt]
\Lambda_{d}^{h}
=\max\!\left[
\tfrac12(\Lambda_{d,L}+\Lambda_{d,R}),
\frac{c_{\mathrm{num}}^2}{A_{l,\mathrm{ref}}}
\right]>0.
\end{gathered}
\label{eq:regular_fluxes}
\end{equation}
Here $\mathbf F_g^{\mathrm{EC}}$ is the entropy-conservative core,
$\mathbf v_g$ is the gas entropy-variable vector, and $\mathbf K_g$ is a
positive-semidefinite characteristic dissipation matrix. The regularization
uses
$A_{l,\mathrm{ref}}=\max[(A_{l,L}+A_{l,R})/2,A_\varepsilon]$, where
$A_\varepsilon>0$ is the area floor, and a positive local speed
$c_{\mathrm{num}}$.
The area-aware gas construction prevents an area jump alone from diffusing gas
mass. The positive $\Lambda_d^h$ regularizes only the local face Riemann
problem; the unmodified $\Lambda_d$ remains the instability diagnostic.
\begin{samepage}
The liquid-full branch uses elastic-pipe speeds $u_p\pm a$. The path
contribution associated with $P_g\partial_xA_g$ is balanced with the same
reconstruction and quadrature.
\end{samepage}

Time integration uses second-order SSP--RK \cite{gottlieb2001}. Reconstruction,
branch fluxes, front traces, and sources are rebuilt at each stage. The step is
limited simultaneously by liquid-full waves, stratified waves, the two cut
subcells, and the time for the front to reach the next face, denoted by
$\Delta t_p$, $\Delta t_f$, $\Delta t_{\mathrm{cut}}$, and $\Delta t_\Gamma$,
respectively:
\begin{equation}
\begin{gathered}
\mathbf U^{(1)}=\mathbf U^n+\Delta t\,\mathbf L(\mathbf U^n),\\[3pt]
\mathbf U^{n+1}=\tfrac12\mathbf U^n
+\tfrac12\left[\mathbf U^{(1)}
+\Delta t\,\mathbf L(\mathbf U^{(1)})\right],\\[3pt]
\Delta t=\min(\Delta t_p,\Delta t_f,
\Delta t_{\mathrm{cut}},\Delta t_\Gamma).
\end{gathered}
\label{eq:ssprk_cfl}
\end{equation}
Here $\mathbf L$ is the semidiscrete spatial operator assembled from the
reconstructed fluxes and sources.

When the front lies inside host cell $j_\Gamma$, the actual liquid-full and
stratified lengths $\ell_p$ and $\ell_f$ are retained. For a control face moving
at $w_\Gamma$, they satisfy
$\ell_p^{n+1}=\ell_p^n+w_\Gamma\Delta t$ and
$\ell_f^{n+1}=\ell_f^n-w_\Gamma\Delta t$ when no face is crossed. With
$\mathbf U_{p,\Gamma}=[A_{p,\Gamma},Q_{p,\Gamma}]^{T}$ and
$\mathbf U_{f,\Gamma}=[A_{l,\Gamma},Q_{f,\Gamma}]^{T}$, define the
relative liquid fluxes
$\mathbf G_{k,\Gamma}=\mathbf F_{k,\Gamma}
-w_\Gamma\mathbf U_{k,\Gamma}$, $k\in\{p,f\}$, and use the shared liquid
flux $\widehat{\mathbf G}_\Gamma
=\tfrac12(\mathbf G_{p,\Gamma}+\mathbf G_{f,\Gamma})$. The subcells obey
\begin{equation}
\begin{gathered}
\ell_p^{n+1}\mathbf U_p^{n+1}
=\ell_p^n\mathbf U_p^n
+\Delta t(\widehat{\mathbf F}_{L}^{p}
-\widehat{\mathbf G}_\Gamma)
+\Delta t\,\ell_p^n\mathbf R_p,\\[3pt]
\ell_f^{n+1}\mathbf U^{n+1}
=\ell_f^n\mathbf U^n
+\Delta t(\widehat{\mathbf G}_\Gamma^{s}
-\widehat{\mathbf F}_{R}^{f})
+\Delta t\,\ell_f^n\mathbf S.
\end{gathered}
\label{eq:cutcell_update}
\end{equation}
Here $\mathbf U$ and $\mathbf S$ in the second line are the complete
four-component stratified state and source. In
$\widehat{\mathbf G}_\Gamma^s$, the liquid entries equal
$\widehat{\mathbf G}_\Gamma$, the gas-mass entry is zero, and the gas-momentum
entry is $P_{g,\Gamma}A_{g,\Gamma}$, representing internal piston action.
Adding the liquid parts cancels the shared relative flux, which gives geometric
conservation as the front moves. The stratified update retains gas mass and
momentum; gas disappears only through a recorded physical boundary flux.

If the front reaches a cell face, the stage is split at the exact arrival time
and the remaining liquid and gas inventories are conservatively remapped to the
new host cell. A remap producing nonpositive gas volume or density, or an
excessive residual, is rejected and repeated with a smaller step; for a
left-moving front, the two orientations are exchanged. Topology changes are
tested only after a positive, conservative step has been accepted. A connected
near-full run becomes a separate
liquid-full segment only when
\begin{equation}
\min_{j_1\le j\le j_2}\frac{A_{l,j}}{A_f}\ge\alpha_b,
\qquad
\sum_{j=j_1}^{j_2}\Delta x_j\ge L_{b,\min}.
\label{eq:topology_criterion}
\end{equation}
Here $\alpha_b=0.98$ and $L_{b,\min}=\max(D,2\Delta x)$. This test identifies
a candidate near-full run but does not prescribe its
speed. The disclosed thresholds are numerical topology criteria, not a
parameter-free wave-breaking or liquid-bridge model. Both bounding
Regime-Transition Riemann Problems must be admissible, and segments merge only
if the intervening gas has a connected escape path.
Formation, merging, splitting, and release inherit the accepted liquid
inventory and redistribute gas only within its connected region. A transaction
that violates liquid mass, liquid momentum, gas inventory, or positivity is
rejected. An under-resolved sealed pocket may use the lumped polytropic closure
$P_gV_g^m=\mathrm{const.}$; resolved stratified gas continues to use the
distributed equations in Eq.~\eqref{eq:system}, where $m$ is the polytropic
exponent.

\subsection{Conservative pipe--shaft junction}
\label{sec:model:junction}
\label{sec:model:riser}

The resolved pipe--shaft connection is treated as a massless, three-branch
finite-volume node rather than as a prescribed source in one horizontal cell.
The left, right, and upward branch coordinates point away from the junction,
and their adjacent liquid traces are denoted by
$(p_b^*,u_b^*,c_b,A_{l,b})$, where
$b\in\mathrm{J}=\{L,R,\mathrm{UP}\}$.
Here $p_b^*$ and $u_b^*=Q_{l,b}^*/A_{l,b}$ are the pressure and outward-velocity
traces obtained from the branch solution.  The incoming liquid celerity is
$c_b=a$ on an elastic liquid-full branch and
$c_b=\sqrt{\Lambda_{d,b}^{h}A_{l,b}}$ on a stratified or free-surface branch,
using the face-regularized coefficient defined in Eq.~\eqref{eq:regular_fluxes}.
The branch orientation and the two phase exchanges are shown in
Figure~\ref{fig:pipe_shaft_junction}.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.88\linewidth]{pipe_shaft_junction_schematic.pdf}
\caption{Pipe--shaft junction discretization. Grey dashed lines mark branch
cell faces, $p_J$ is a zero-storage node, and blue double-headed arrows denote
signed liquid discharge.}
\label{fig:pipe_shaft_junction}
\end{figure}

For a trial absolute pressure $p_J$ at the common node datum, each incoming
characteristic supplies its own signed branch velocity and physical minor loss:
\begin{equation}
\begin{gathered}
\frac{p_{l,J,b}-p_b^*}{\rho_l}
=c_b\bigl(u_{J,b}^{\mathrm{out}}-u_b^*\bigr)
+\frac{K_b}{2}u_{J,b}^{\mathrm{out}}
 \left|u_{J,b}^{\mathrm{out}}\right|,\\[3pt]
p_{l,J,b}=p_J+\delta p_{h,b},
\qquad
Q_{l,J,b}^{\mathrm{out}}=A_{l,b}u_{J,b}^{\mathrm{out}},\\[3pt]
\sum_{b\in\mathrm{J}}Q_{l,J,b}^{\mathrm{out}}=0.
\end{gathered}
\label{eq:junction_balance}
\end{equation}
Here $\delta p_{h,b}$ is the hydrostatic pressure offset between the common
node datum and branch $b$, and $K_b$ is its local loss coefficient.  Because
each characteristic relation is monotone in $p_J$, the scalar residual in the
last line of Eq.~\eqref{eq:junction_balance} is bracketed and solved at every
Runge--Kutta stage.  No sign restriction is imposed on the resulting flows;
water may therefore enter the shaft or return naturally to either horizontal
branch.  The three computed node fluxes replace the ordinary numerical fluxes
at the corresponding branch faces.  In the conservative pressure-potential
gauge used by the liquid solver, the inserted face flux is
\begin{equation}
\widehat{\mathbf F}_{l,J,b}=
\begin{bmatrix}
Q_{l,J,b}^{\mathrm{out}}\\[2pt]
\bigl(Q_{l,J,b}^{\mathrm{out}}\bigr)^2/A_{l,b}
+\Psi_b^*+(p_{l,J,b}-p_b^*)A_{l,b}/\rho_l
\end{bmatrix}.
\label{eq:junction_liquid_flux}
\end{equation}
Here $\Psi_b^*$ is the adjacent branch value of the liquid pressure potential
in the regular momentum flux
$F_{Q_l}=Q_l^2/A_l+\Psi_l$.  Thus
$\Psi_{J,b}=\Psi_b^*+(p_{l,J,b}-p_b^*)A_{l,b}/\rho_l$ uses the same pressure
gauge as the interior liquid operator.  This pressure-difference form retains
the full advective and pressure work but does not introduce an impulse into a
uniform hydrostatic state.

Gas exchange is activated only when the resolved crown gas exposes a finite
part of the shaft mouth.  Its open area $A_{g,J}$ is obtained from the geometric
overlap of the horizontal gas layer and the shaft bore.  To distinguish the
mouth problem from the area-weighted parent state in Eq.~\eqref{eq:system}, let
$\mathbf q_{g,b}^*=[\rho_{g,b}^*,\rho_{g,b}^*u_{g,n,b}^*]^{\mathsf T}$ denote
the intrinsic gas state per unit mouth area, where $u_{g,n,b}^*$ is normal to
the mouth.  The per-unit-area isothermal gas Riemann flux
$\widehat{\mathbf f}_g$ then supplies the normal mass and momentum exchange:
\begin{equation}
\begin{gathered}
\begin{bmatrix}\dot m_{g,J}\\ \dot I_{g,\mathrm{UP},J}\end{bmatrix}
=A_{g,J}\widehat{\mathbf f}_g
 \left(\mathbf q_{g,h}^*,\mathbf q_{g,\mathrm{UP}}^*\right),\\[3pt]
\Delta M_{g,h,J}=-\Delta t\,\dot m_{g,J},\\[3pt]
\Delta M_{g,\mathrm{UP},1}=+\Delta t\,\dot m_{g,J},\\[3pt]
\Delta J_{g,\mathrm{UP},1}=+\Delta t\,\dot I_{g,\mathrm{UP},J}.
\end{gathered}
\label{eq:junction_gas_flux}
\end{equation}
Here $M_{g,h,J}$ is the cell-integrated gas mass in the horizontal junction
cell, whereas $M_{g,\mathrm{UP},1}$ is that in the first vertical cell.
The quantity $J_{g,\mathrm{UP},1}$ is the vertical gas-momentum inventory.
Accordingly,
$\dot m_{g,J}$ is the gas mass rate from the horizontal branch into the shaft
and $\dot I_{g,\mathrm{UP},J}$ is its resolved vertical momentum-flux rate.
The horizontal axial velocity is tangential to this mouth, so its normal gas
trace is zero; the vertical trace comes from the first shaft cell.  Gas mass is
subtracted from the horizontal inventory and added to the shaft inventory in
one residual evaluation.  Momentum remains directional at the $90^{\circ}$
turn: $\dot I_{g,\mathrm{UP},J}$ updates vertical momentum, horizontal axial momentum
retains its own advective flux, and their vector difference is the reaction on
the fitting.  It would therefore be incorrect to transfer one scalar momentum
flux unchanged between the two axes.  Donor positivity is enforced by time
step subdivision rather than by prescribing a transfer fraction.  Both phase
exchanges are internal conservative fluxes, not post-step source terms.
